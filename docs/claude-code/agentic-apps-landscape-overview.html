Who's Building Agentic Apps — and How

A plain-language map of the companies and tools building software that gives users freedom through AI agents, ranked by scale, with three standout projects examined up close. Written for people who know AI but don't write code.

Compiled 2026-05-26 · Figures gathered from company sites, press, and live GitHub counts · Numbers move fast and several are "reported / in talks" — see footnotes & sources.

★ Start here — the 60-second version

"Agentic apps" are tools where an AI does multi-step work on your behalf — not just answering, but acting: writing & running code, browsing the web, resolving support tickets, organizing files. The market split into four buckets:

  • Coding agents — the biggest commercial winners so far (Cursor, Devin, Replit, Claude Code).
  • Build-your-own frameworks — open-source toolkits devs use to assemble agents (LangChain, CrewAI, n8n, Dify).
  • Computer/browser-use agents — AI that drives a real screen (ChatGPT Agent, Manus, Browser Use).
  • Vertical enterprise agents — agents sold to a specific industry, where the real money is (Sierra=support, Harvey=legal, Glean=search).

If you read nothing else, jump to the three standouts: Cursor (fastest-growing software company ever), Manus (the surprise viral "general agent"), and Sierra (agents that only get paid when they solve your problem).

3 · Three standouts, up close

Chosen for variety and significance: the breakout commercial success, the biggest surprise, and the most forward-looking business model.

🏆 Cursor — the breakout success most successful

Anysphere · AI code editor · closed-source

Cursor is an AI-first code editor where an agent reads your whole project, proposes multi-file changes, and runs "background agents" that work while you do something else. It became the fastest software company in history to reach $2B in annual revenue, reportedly going from ~$100M to ~$2B ARR in about a year.

~$29.3B valuation¹~$2B ARR$20–200/mo
Why it matters: Cursor is the proof that agentic apps are a real, enormous market — not hype. It also shows the winning consumer playbook: take an everyday workflow (writing code), make the agent feel like a natural extension of it, and charge a simple subscription.

🚀 Manus — the surprise most surprising

Butterfly Effect / Monica (China) · general autonomous agent · closed-source

Manus went viral in March 2025 as a "general agent" that takes a goal, spins up its own virtual computer, and completes the entire multi-step task — research, building files, booking things — then hands you the finished result. It reportedly hit ~$100M revenue in about 8 months (among the fastest ever) and was reportedly acquired by Meta in Dec 2025 for $2B+¹.

~$100M ARR in 8moreported Meta acq. $2B+¹2M+ week-1 waitlist
Why it matters: Manus is the clearest bet on the "do the whole job for me" future — and a reminder that a breakout agentic app can now come from anywhere and scale in months. (The Meta deal price is press-reported, not officially confirmed.)

🔮 Sierra — the forward-looking model most forward-looking

Bret Taylor (ex-Salesforce co-CEO, OpenAI chair) · enterprise customer-experience agents · closed-source

Sierra builds customer-service agents that resolve issues end-to-end for big companies. Its standout idea is outcome-based pricing: instead of charging per seat or per message, Sierra largely charges per problem actually resolved. It reached ~$150M ARR and a ~$15B valuation in under two years, serving 40%+ of the Fortune 50.

~$15B valuation~$150M ARRpay-per-resolution
Why it matters: "Pay only when the agent succeeds" may be the pricing model the whole industry moves toward — it aligns cost with value and is only possible because the agent does real work, not just assist. Worth watching for how we'd ever price an Atram agent.

Honorable mentions: Devin (the autonomous-engineer bet), Browser Use (a tiny open-source project at ~96k stars powering computer-use everywhere), and MCP — Anthropic's open "plug" standard that lets any agent connect to any tool (now an industry default).

The through-line: the winners pick a clear job, let the AI do it end-to-end, and price around the value delivered. Cursor proves the market is huge, Manus shows breakout speed is possible from anywhere, and Sierra shows where pricing is heading. Note all three are closed-source products — the open-source world (Section 2) is mostly the picks-and-shovels layer that everyone, including these companies, builds on.

5 · What it means for Atram

1. The market is proven and fast. Agentic apps went from curiosity to multi-billion-dollar businesses in ~18 months. Speed-to-traction is unusually high — the bar for "interesting" is now "does the whole job," not "assists with it."

2. Pick a job, own it end-to-end. Every winner is built around one clear outcome (write code / resolve a ticket / do the task). For our own products, the lesson is to define the outcome sharply and let the agent own the full loop — the Every "agent-native" idea, validated at scale.

3. Watch outcome-based pricing. Sierra's "pay when it works" model is the most forward-looking commercial idea here and likely where client expectations head next.

4. Build on the open layer. We don't need to reinvent the plumbing — LangChain, the MCP standard, Browser Use, and sandbox runtimes (E2B) are mature, free building blocks. Buy/borrow the picks-and-shovels; spend our effort on the specific outcome we deliver.
